# Prior-art check, claim by claim (2026-10-06)

*Compiled by a research sub-agent: full texts on PMC; OpenAlex cited-by screening of MINDFUL (18 works) and Wilson 2025 (9);
Europe PMC, arXiv and the web.*

## Verdicts

| Claim | Verdict | Key prior work | How to phrase it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **C1** Decay time course conserved across monkeys and labs, plus an oracle ladder | PARTIALLY KNOWN | LINK (Temmar 2025) already shows monkey N's ridge/LSTM decay curves. Gallego 2020 and Ma 2023 show fixed-decoder decline in Chewie/Mihili. **Wilson 2025: T5 R² 0.39 → 0.21 at 1–2 weeks (about −46%)**, which is human corroboration. FALCON uses data-use tiers. Counterpoints: Bishop 2014 (no progressive worsening over 48–58 d), Flint 2013, Chestek 2011 | "Decay has been reported in individual datasets; we show its time course is quantitatively similar across animals and labs, and decompose the loss." Report absolute R² near 4 months too |
| **C2a** Label-free alignment ≤ renormalization | **CONTESTED** | Wilson 2025: FA stabilizer, ADAN and PRI-T beat *mean-only* recalibration on T5. Degenhart 2020: the stabilizer helped online. Ma 2023: PAF, ADAN and Cycle-GAN beat a *fixed* decoder on C and M. NoMAD 2025. FALCON: alignment < static Wiener filter on H1, but > on M1 and M2 | Frame it as a **baseline effect**: prior baselines were un-normalized or mean-only. Our data agree that L4u > L1 (recentre, i.e. mean-only) but L4u ≤ L2 (full per-channel z-scoring). Test directly against mean-only recalibration |
| **C2b** Label-free statistics add nothing beyond elapsed time | **NEW** | Pun 2024 (MINDFUL): raw r = 0.93 (T11) and 0.72 (T5), with no partial correlation or elapsed-time baseline. It notes that larger time gaps give larger KL divergence, and that fixed-decoder performance sometimes recovered. Wilson 2025: HMM likelihood r = 0.50, also time-uncontrolled. **None of 18 MINDFUL citers or 9 Wilson citers controls for time** | "Prior label-free metrics were validated by raw correlation; we test incremental validity beyond time since calibration." Scope: offline, between-session data. Re-test on MINDFUL's own data |
| **C3** Per-channel gains explain about 15–50% of loss | NEW (quantitative) | Perge 2013 (within-day changes); **Bishop 2014: same-electrode tuning parameters move together between days (gain/offset-like), which is in tension with C3**; Degenhart 2020 taxonomy; Jarosiewicz 2015 bias correction; Wilson 2025: mean-only recalibration reaches 0.78 at short gaps | Report the gain-explained fraction as a function of days apart; discuss Bishop 2014 |
| **C4** Regularize for the future (same-day CV under-regularizes) | **NEW for iBCI** (principle partly known) | No intracortical paper on ridge strength vs cross-day robustness. Sussillo 2016 (more data plus perturbations); Shaikh 2020 TNSRE (channel bagging). ML/EEG: Sugiyama, Krauledat & Müller 2007 JMLR (CV is biased under session shift); Samek 2012; Lotte & Guan 2011. **Patil, Du & Tibshirani ICML 2024: the optimal ridge under shift can move either way**, so ours is an empirical finding | "To our knowledge, the first demonstration in iBCIs that same-day CV picks a ridge penalty orders of magnitude below the future-optimal one." Report absolute R² (the 2.6× is a ratio of small numbers) |
| **C5** Ridge-to-prior with history-chosen strength | PARTIALLY KNOWN (estimator known) | SmoothBatch/CLDA (Orsborn 2012; Dangi 2013); Bayesian UKF updates (Li 2011); Bishop 2014 priors; Hsieh & Shanechi 2018 (learning-rate calibration). **Kuzborskij & Orabona ICML 2013: biased regularization toward a source, with parameters by LOO, i.e. essentially our estimator.** BCI multitask priors (Alamgir 2010; Jayaram 2016) | "A simple, principled recipe and its systematic characterization": multi-year sample-efficiency curves and **harmful-recalibration rates (not found in prior iBCI work)**. Not a new algorithm |
| **C6a** About 80% of silenced electrodes revive; Markov switching | **NEW** | Hahn 2025: anecdotes only (pedestal repair or trauma); Barrese 2013 (connector shorts); Dunlap 2020 ("may resolve spontaneously", no rates) | Rule out connector, pedestal and threshold artifacts explicitly |
| **C6b** Heavy-tailed abrupt single-electrode changes | PARTIALLY KNOWN | Perge 2013 (within-day); Degenhart 2020 | The across-day, heavy-tailed characterization is new |
| **C6c** Edge electrodes lose spiking faster; SBP declines uniformly | PARTIALLY KNOWN | **Forrest 2025 JNE**: FEM strain is highest at edges and correlates with impedance and SNR in 4 human arrays, but the direction reverses in monkey V4. **Patel 2023 JNE**: outer shanks have more coating cracks and tip breakage. **Barrese 2016**: no spatial difference in SEM. Hahn 2025: qualitative edge impedance. LINK: qualitatively lower activity on lateral channels | New: a time-to-loss test across 20 human arrays plus a monkey (Fisher p = 2e-8), and the dissociation that spiking loss is edge-biased while SBP declines uniformly |
| **C6d** Impedance falls over years | ALREADY PUBLISHED | Hahn 2025; Chen 2023; Bjånes 2025; Forrest 2025 | Present as replication |
| **C7** Calibrated simulator; drift slows later in implant life | **NEW** | Wilson 2025 closed-loop simulator (exponential subspace drift fitted to 2 weeks of T5, no held-out validation); Wan 2023 (uncalibrated); Degenhart 2020 and Sussillo 2016 perturbations; "Neural Digital Twins" arXiv 2601.01539 (perspective) | No precedent found for "drift slows later". It is consistent with Chestek 2011 (early amplitude loss) and Hahn 2025 (slow late yield decline) |
